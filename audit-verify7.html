Public audit

A fact-checking guide, fact-checked

10 claims from the ARINTECHNO VERIFY-7 guide, checked claim by claim against the sources the guide itself cites. 9 verified, 1 unverifiable, nothing broken — published 17 November 2026.

In short: 10 claims from the ARINTECHNO VERIFY-7 fact-checking guide, checked claim by claim against the sources the guide itself cites. 9 verified (90%), 1 unverifiable (10%), 0 broken, 0 mismatch, 0 no URL. Published 17 November 2026. Reviewed by Riya Nalwa, co-founder, before publication.

Total: 10 claims from one guide. The audited document is ARINTECHNO’s “How to Fact-Check AI Answers (VERIFY-7)” (arintechnow.com), sources last checked per the guide: 15 November 2026. Audit run: 17 November 2026.

How it was done

10 factual claims extracted from the guide; each checked claim by claim against the source the guide itself cites (primary source opened and read, not a snippet). Verdict taxonomy: Verified / Broken / Mismatch / Unverifiable / No URL — the Oryx standard. No client material involved; all sources public. A fact-checking guide is the fairest first public audit — it makes checkable claims, names its sources, and its subject is verification itself. Auditing it tests Oryx’s method in public without touching a competitor or a client.

The evidence, claim by claim

Claim 1 — Verified

Claim (as written): “OpenAI explicitly warns that models can produce wrong facts, fabricated quotes, nonexistent studies, and invented references.”

Source cited: OpenAI Help, “Does ChatGPT tell the truth?” (help.openai.com).

Evidence pointer: OpenAI help page opened and read: “it can produce incorrect or misleading outputs” — “Incorrect definitions, dates, or facts”; “Fabricated quotes, studies, citations or references to non-existent sources”. The guide’s wording is an accurate paraphrase of the page’s list.

Limitation: Paraphrase, not a verbatim quote — the substance matches exactly.

Claim 2 — Verified

Claim (as written): “Its Search documentation also notes that search results and citations can be incomplete, outdated, or incorrect.”

Source cited: OpenAI Help, “Searching the web with ChatGPT” (help.openai.com).

Evidence pointer: OpenAI help page opened and read: verbatim — “Search results and citations can be incomplete, outdated, or incorrect.” Word-for-word match.

Limitation: None.

Claim 3 — Verified

Claim (as written): “Google similarly warns that Gemini may omit a source it used, cite something that does not directly support the claim, or in some cases present a made-up source.”

Source cited: Google Workspace Learning Center, “Learn how to use sources with Gemini” (support.google.com).

Evidence pointer: Google support page opened and read: “Gemini might miss a source it used, or it may cite a document that is not directly used for a specific piece of information. In some cases, Gemini can make up a source, presenting it as factual even if it wasn’t part of the request.” Word-for-word match.

Limitation: None.

Claim 4 — Verified

Claim (as written): “A peer-reviewed study published on November 13, 2026 tested four contemporary models across 80 responses: 40% contained citation-related problems and another 7.5% had no usable references.”

Source cited: BMC Research Notes, “When AI invents evidence” (link.springer.com), DOI 10.1186/s13104-026-08129-2.

Evidence pointer: Springer abstract opened and read: 80 responses; 32 (40.0%) citation-related problems; 6 (7.5%) no usable references; 42 (52.5%) satisfactory. Four models: GPT-5.6, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude Opus 4.6, Grok 4.5. Published 13 Nov 2026. Numbers match exactly.

Limitation: “Peer-reviewed” is the journal’s character (BMC Research Notes is a peer-reviewed journal); the abstract itself does not restate the peer-review status.

Claim 5 — Verified

Claim (as written): “Matter 1.6 is a focused feature release and, unlike 1.5, does not expand into new device categories.”

Source cited: Connectivity Standards Alliance — Matter 1.6 (csa-iot.org).

Evidence pointer: CSA primary announcement opened and read 17 Nov 2026 (csa-iot.org/newsroom/connectivity-standards-alliance-kicks-off-unify/, 17 Jun 2026): “This focused feature release enhances how devices interact across ecosystems, improves their ability to adapt to user preferences, and provides a deeper understanding of device status” — no new device categories are announced. The “no new device categories” wording is confirmed by two independent secondary sources quoting the CSA Matter 1.6 page verbatim: “Rather than expanding into new device categories, this release enhances device setup”. Both agree Matter 1.6 (17 Jun 2026) added NFC commissioning and Joint Fabric, no new device categories.

Limitation: Primary announcement opened 17 Nov 2026 (gap closed). The exact “no new device categories” phrasing is confirmed via secondary sources quoting the CSA Matter 1.6 page; the announcement itself describes a focused feature release and lists no new categories.

Claim 6 — Verified

Claim (as written): “Apple explicitly warns against ambient temperatures above 35°C and says they can permanently reduce battery lifespan.”

Source cited: Apple, “Batteries — Maximizing Performance” (apple.com).

Evidence pointer: Apple page opened and read: “It’s especially important to avoid exposing your device to ambient temperatures higher than 35° C (95° F), which can permanently damage battery capacity. That is, your battery won’t power your device as long on a given charge.” Substance matches.

Limitation: Wording note: Apple says “permanently damage battery capacity”; the guide renders it as “permanently reduce battery lifespan”. Same meaning, different words — recorded, not treated as a mismatch.

Claim 7 — Verified

Claim (as written): “OpenAI says ChatGPT cannot reliably determine whether text is AI-generated or whether it wrote a particular text.”

Source cited: OpenAI Help, “Does ChatGPT tell the truth?” (help.openai.com).

Evidence pointer: Same OpenAI page: “ChatGPT can’t reliably determine whether text was AI-generated or whether it wrote the text.” Word-for-word match.

Limitation: None.

Claim 8 — Verified

Claim (as written): “Research on lateral-reading instruction has repeatedly found improvements in digital source evaluation.”

Source cited: Cognitive Research: Principles and Implications (PMC).

Evidence pointer: ERIC record EJ1292610 opened and read: Brodsky et al., Cognitive Research 2021, v6 Article 23 — students taught lateral reading “were more likely to use lateral reading to fact-check and correctly evaluate the trustworthiness of information than controls”. Wineburg & McGrew (2019) is the underlying lateral-reading work, also found in the literature.

Limitation: “Repeatedly” rests on the wider literature (Wineburg & McGrew 2019 + Brodsky 2021); the guide cites one paper directly.

Claim 9 — Unverifiable

Claim (as written): “Sources last checked: November 15, 2026.”

Source cited: The guide itself — no external source.

Evidence pointer: No check log is published on the page, so the act of checking cannot be verified from the document itself. The date is consistent with the contents (the guide cites the BMC study published 13 Nov 2026), but consistency is not verification of the act.

Limitation: The claim is about the author’s process, not a checkable fact. A published check log would make it verifiable.

Claim 10 — Verified

Claim (as written): NIST “Generative AI Profile: Confabulation” is cited as a source.

Source cited: NIST AI 600-1 (nvlpubs.nist.gov), DOI 10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1.

Evidence pointer: NIST publication opened and read: “Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile” (NIST AI 600-1, July 2024). Section 2: “Confabulation: The production of confidently stated but erroneous or false content (known colloquially as ‘hallucinations’ or ‘fabrications’)”. The publication exists and covers confabulation exactly as cited.

Limitation: None.

Findings

  • 9 of 10 claims verified (90%). The guide is careful: its vendor-warning claims are word-for-word accurate against the vendors’ own help pages, and its headline numbers (40% / 7.5%) match the BMC study abstract exactly.
  • The one gap is a process claim, not a factual one. “Sources last checked: November 15, 2026” cannot be verified because no check log is published. That is the same gap Oryx’s own method closes with a dated evidence table and a verification page — and it is why the audit recommends publishing the check log, not just the date.
  • One wording note, not a failure. Apple’s “permanently damage battery capacity” becomes “permanently reduce battery lifespan” in the guide. Same meaning; recorded so the reader sees the exact words.
  • One verification gap of our own — now closed. The CSA primary announcement was opened directly on 17 Nov 2026 and confirms the “focused feature release” wording with no new device categories listed; the exact “no new device categories” phrasing is corroborated by two independent secondary sources quoting the CSA Matter 1.6 page.

What this audit shows

  • A good guide survives a claim-by-claim check: 90% verified, nothing broken, nothing mismatched. That is what “every claim checked, every check shown” looks like on someone else’s work.
  • The method works on a short document in hours, not days — the same method the pilot sells, at the same standard (every claim, every check, gaps labelled).

Limits of this audit (stated, not hidden)

  • 10 claims from one short guide — a small sample, not a judgement on the whole site or on ARINTECHNO generally.
  • Claim 5’s “no new device categories” phrasing is corroborated via secondary sources quoting the CSA Matter 1.6 page; the CSA primary announcement was opened directly on 17 Nov 2026 and confirms the substance (focused feature release, no new categories listed).
  • No contact was made with ARINTECHNO; no response was sought. If they correct or dispute anything, the audit is updated and the correction is disclosed.
