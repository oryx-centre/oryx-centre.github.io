Evidence Audit and Vendor-Claim Verification

Check an AI-generated report, a draft, or a vendor's claims pack — claim by claim — before you rely on it.

Fluency is not evidence. A fluent paragraph can cite a source that does not exist, does not match, or does not support the claim. This service retraces each citation and counts it only when the source actually supports the claim.

Verification is built into the delivery by default — not something you scramble to do after an error is found.

The same method now covers AI governance — guardrails, safety, security and compliance mapping for organisations and governments. See below.

What we check

1. Evidence Audit — for organisations

An audit of an AI-generated research report or a client draft: every material claim is traced to its cited source, and each citation is reported as verified, broken, title-mismatched, or unverifiable. Gaps and confidence are stated, not smoothed over.

2. Vendor-Claim Verification — for organisations

The same check, scoped to one vendor's claims pack: the vendor's claims are checked against published evidence, claim by claim, using the same retracing method. This service does not certify a vendor — it reports what the evidence does and does not support.

We do not offer general strategy, implementation, training, or advisory retainers. Desk research only.

AI governance services — for organisations and governments

The same evidence method, applied wider: every governance claim about an AI system is checked claim by claim against evidence, and you get a written, evidence-backed report.

3. Guardrails Evaluation

We test whether an AI system's guardrails hold in practice — refusal behaviour where refusal is required, policy compliance of outputs, and resistance to jailbreak attempts. Run on your system with your permission, with every test logged with its evidence.

4. AI Safety Audit

Evidence checks on what a deployed system actually produces: hallucination rate on a sample, factual accuracy of outputs, and bias spot-checks on defined test sets. Measured rates with the underlying evidence, not assertions.

5. AI Security Review

We test an AI deployment for prompt-injection resistance, data leakage, and access-control behaviour, with your permission only. Each test is reported with whether it succeeded and the evidence.

6. Governance and Compliance Mapping

We map your AI deployment against the frameworks that apply to it — the EU AI Act, the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, OECD AI Principles, India's DPDP Act and AI governance guidelines, or your own national framework — clause by clause: what is covered, what is missing, and what evidence exists for each item. This is a gap analysis, not legal advice and not a certification of compliance.

Guardrails, safety, security and compliance work is offered as pilots under the same terms below. The citation audit is our published, proven method; these lines are new, and a first pilot in each is what proves it. Desk research and permissioned testing only.

How it works

  1. You send the material and the question. One report, one draft, one vendor's claims pack, or one governance pilot scope, with the question it is supposed to answer.
  2. We agree the pass criteria in writing, before any work. What counts as a cited, checkable claim, the scope and format, and what counts as a fail are written down and signed — they are not agreed by talking about them.
  3. We check it, then a human reviews it. The method is: question framing, evidence acquisition, claim-level traceability, adversarial review, human decision gates, and monitoring and correction. No report leaves without human review.
  4. You confirm acceptance or failure in writing within 5 working days of delivery. If it fails a criterion, one revision round is included.

What you get

  • A report or audit with every material claim sourced and cited.
  • A sourced-claim rate, gaps, and confidence stated plainly.
  • An Audit Certificate as page 1 of the delivered report.
  • A clear statement of what the evidence does NOT show, where that applies.
  • For a governance pilot: a report listing each test or framework item, what held, what broke or is missing, and the evidence for each.
