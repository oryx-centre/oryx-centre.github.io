Burning Activity Calendar — Punjab, India, October 2025

NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 · Challenge: Harmonization of MODIS and VIIRS Hot Spots · Team: Oryx AI (solo, Amartya Kumar, amartya-oryxai) · Data: NASA FIRMS, 2025 archive

The headline

The two satellite records barely agree with each other — which is exactly the problem this challenge states. In Punjab in October 2025, VIIRS saw 1,902 fires and MODIS saw 927, but only 102 were the same fire seen by both sensors. The harmonized record — the union of both — contains 2,727 distinct fire events. Either sensor alone understates the burning season; MODIS alone would miss more than half of it.

Fire radiative power totals: VIIRS 10,107.0 MW, MODIS 13,115.8 MW. MODIS reports more total energy despite fewer detections, because each 1 km MODIS pixel aggregates a larger burning area than a 375 m VIIRS pixel.

Critical period: the burning season surged in the last two weeks. Median day: 32 VIIRS detections. Days above twice the median: 10-18, 10-19, 10-20, 10-21, 10-22, 10-25, 10-26, 10-27, 10-29, 10-30, 10-31. Peak day 2025-10-31 with 313 VIIRS detections.

How much do the sensors agree? (sensitivity to matching tolerance)

Matching rule: same calendar date AND within the stated distance; greedy one-to-one, MODIS by descending FRP, nearest unmatched VIIRS wins. The finding does not depend on the tolerance — agreement stays low at every distance, so the split record is real, not a parameter artefact.

Method

  1. Downloaded NASA FIRMS country archive files for India, 2025 (VIIRS S-NPP and MODIS C6.1), no key required.
  2. Filtered to Punjab (lon 73.8–76.9, lat 29.5–32.5) and October 2025, peak stubble-burning season.
  3. Matched detections across sensors by date + distance; unmatched detections kept as sensor-only.
  4. Built the calendar from the union of both records, with per-day counts and fire radiative power.

Limitations — stated plainly

  • Same-day matching ignores overpass-time differences (VIIRS ~13:00 IST, MODIS Aqua ~14:30 IST); stubble fires burn for hours, so many fires are genuinely seen by one sensor only.
  • A fixed distance box can merge distinct nearby fires and split one large fire seen as several VIIRS pixels.
  • MODIS (1 km) misses small fires VIIRS (375 m) detects; agreement rates measure cross-sensor consistency, not ground truth.
  • Archive 2025 data, not live near-real-time. A live version would use the FIRMS API with a MAP_KEY.
