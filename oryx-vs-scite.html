Oryx Evidence Audit vs Scite.ai

Scite is the index. Oryx is the audit. A sourced comparison of claim-level verification against citation-index scale — including where Scite wins.

This page compares Oryx Evidence Audit with Scite.ai using Scite’s own published sources. Where Scite wins, it says so. Figures checked 20 November 2026.

Scite is the index. Oryx is the audit. Scite tells you how the literature has cited a paper. Oryx tells you whether the claim in front of you is true to its own source. Those are different jobs — and on the job “is this claim supported by the source it cites”, Oryx checks deeper. On the job “map the whole literature”, Scite covers wider by orders of magnitude.

What Scite does

Scite (founded 2018, Brooklyn; acquired by Research Solutions in 2023 for $20.9 million) built Smart Citations: a deep-learning model that classifies each citation statement as supporting, contrasting, or mentioning, and shows the citation sentence, its surrounding context, and retraction or editorial notices. Its database holds 323 million indexed articles, 50 million in full text, 1.7 billion Smart Citations and 2.9 billion unique citations, built with 44 publisher agreements (coverage page, September 2026). Note that Scite’s own homepage and pricing page round these figures down to 300 million+ and 1.6 billion+. Its Reference Check takes a manuscript PDF and reports how the manuscript cites its own references, flagging retractions and editorial notices. Its Assistant answers research questions grounded in the citation database. Basic costs $12 a month billed annually ($144 a year) or $20 a month; Pro costs $40 a month billed annually ($480 a year) or $50 a month; Team is $40–50 per seat; Enterprise is custom. There is a 7-day free trial and no permanent free plan.

Scite’s own published paper reports its classifier’s accuracy: on a 9,708-example holdout set, the production model reached an F-score of 0.590 for contrasting citations (precision 0.852, recall 0.451), 0.648 for supporting, and 0.973 for mentioning; in production every class is balanced to precision above 80%. The same paper records a limit: ingestion misses about 30% of citation statements in PDFs. The one independent test (Bakker et al. 2023) found lower accuracy — F-measures of 0.0 to 0.58, with Scite labelling 96 of 98 citations “mentioning” where human reviewers found 42 supporting and 17 contrasting — and Scite’s 2025 reply disputes that test’s definitions, so the accuracy literature is unsettled; no newer independent audit was found.

What Oryx does

Oryx Evidence Audit breaks a document into its individual claims, resolves each cited source, and checks whether the source supports the exact claim — one verdict per claim: verified, broken, mismatch, unverifiable, or no URL. In its 194-citation audit of 30 AI-generated research briefs, 50 citations verified, 95 were broken, 17 mismatched, 31 unverifiable, and 1 had no URL. In its 66-citation study, only 5 claims were both resolved and fully supported. Every delivered report is human-reviewed before delivery, carries an Audit Certificate, and is sealed with a SHA-256 hash you can verify. The free check covers 5 citations; the pilot costs ₹7,500 in India or $150 abroad, payable only on acceptance.

Social: followers are not the contest

Scite has about 28,000 Instagram followers (stated on indexed posts from January–February 2026; the November count could not be verified — its profile redirects to a login wall), 5,810 LinkedIn followers verified live, 4,230 YouTube subscribers across 44 videos, and accounts on X, TikTok, Facebook and Threads. Its X follower count could not be verified (x.com returned an access error) and no TikTok count was found, so its total verifiable social audience is about 38,000 against 2–3 million claimed users. Oryx has 0 Instagram followers, 26 LinkedIn page followers, and no YouTube channel yet. Scite wins every follower count. But its recent Instagram Reels draw 8 to 14 likes on 28,000 followers — about 0.03–0.05% per post — its LinkedIn posts draw 4 to 29 reactions, and its YouTube videos mostly draw 50 to 600 views; its posts are feature announcements, not proof. Oryx’s social bar is different: beat Scite on engagement rate and on proof-led content — hooks, real audit numbers, live receipts, and a path from a comment to a free check to a pilot — not on follower count in 15 days.

Where Scite wins

Scale, speed, published accuracy data, institutional adoption, and every social follower count. If you need to map how the whole literature has received a paper, or you need an answer in seconds, Scite is the tool. Oryx does not pretend otherwise.

Where Oryx wins

Depth per claim, human review, a sealed and verifiable report, pay-only-on-acceptance pricing, checking AI-generated research, and Global South fit. If you need to know whether the claims in a document are true to their sources — and to prove it, claim by claim, to someone else — that is the audit.
