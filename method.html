Method

The Oryx Evidence Standard

Exactly how the 194-citation audit was done — the taxonomy, the sample, the evidence table — published so anyone can repeat it or dispute it.

In short: every material claim is checked on its own — resolve the citation, open the source, read it against the claim — and given one of five verdicts. The method, the denominator and the dispute are all published. The method is licensed CC BY 4.0, like the papers.

The frame: Check–Show–Stand

Every Oryx Evidence Audit runs on one frame — Check–Show–Stand. Check every material claim on its own: resolve the citation, open the source, read it against the claim. Show every check: the verdict, the evidence and the reason, in an evidence table a reader can re-perform. Stand: the report stands up to challenge — any check can be disputed and is re-examined with the evidence shown. The frame echoes the promise: every claim checked, every check shown.

Every report follows the same six sections: scope (what was checked), criteria (what counts as passing), method (how each claim was checked), findings (the verdicts, with evidence), limitations (what could not be checked), and conclusion (what the findings add up to).

How the 194-citation audit was done

The audit is published as Paper 2 v2, Trust, But Verify (Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23731235), and the method itself is set out in Working Paper v3, From Answers to Auditable Intelligence (Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23718472). In outline:

  1. Collect the sample. 30 AI research briefs, and every citation in them — 194 citations in total. The denominator is the whole set, not a selection.
  2. Resolve each citation. Follow the link or reference to its source. A citation that cannot be reached is recorded as such, not skipped.
  3. Match the source to the claim. Open the source and read what it actually says. A real source that does not support the claim is a different failure from a broken link, and is counted differently.
  4. Assign one verdict per citation. Exactly one of the five verdicts below — no partial credit, no averaging away failures.
  5. Record the evidence. Every verdict sits in an evidence table with the claim, the citation as given, the source as found, and the reason for the verdict.
  6. Human review. The draft audit passes a human review gate before it is published or delivered.

The verdict taxonomy

  • Verified. The source resolves, exists, and supports the claim it is attached to. 50 of 194 (26%).
  • Broken. The link does not resolve to a retrievable source. 95 of 194 (49%).
  • Title mismatch. The source exists, but its title — or what it actually says — does not match the citation as given. 17 of 194 (9%).
  • No URL. The citation carries no link or locator at all. 1 of 194.
  • Unverifiable. The citation cannot be confirmed or denied from what is available — the source cannot be found, or what it says cannot be checked. 31 of 194 (16%).

Some claims cannot be verified. Those are marked, not guessed — an unverifiable verdict is a finding, not a gap in the method.

The evidence table

Every audit ends in an evidence table: one row per claim, with the claim, the citation as given, the source as found, the verdict, and the reason. Every cell links to its source passage, so a reader can re-perform any check without taking our word for it. A report without an evidence table is not an audit — it is an assertion.

Provenance

Every report carries a provenance block: what was checked, when, against which sources, by which method version, and who reviewed it. Every exported report is sealed with a SHA-256 hash of its text and its PDF, printed in the report footer, so a reader can check that the report they hold is the report that was delivered.

Denominator discipline

Publish the method, the denominator and the dispute. The denominator is the whole set that was checked — 194 citations, not the 50 that passed. A rate without a denominator is not evidence. And every output is contestable: challenge any check, and it is re-examined with the evidence shown.

Every audit sharpens the next

That is method language, not a data claim: each audit’s verdicts, failure patterns and disputed checks feed back into the checklist the next audit runs against. The method improves; your material is never reused, never used for training, and never shared.
