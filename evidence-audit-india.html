Evidence Audit and Vendor-Claim Verification

Check an AI-generated report, a draft, or a vendor's claims pack — claim by claim — before you rely on it.

Fluency is not evidence. A fluent paragraph can cite a source that does not exist, does not match, or does not support the claim. This service retraces each citation and counts it only when the source actually supports the claim.

Verification is built into the delivery by default — not something you scramble to do after an error is found.

What we check

1. Evidence Audit — for organisations

An audit of an AI-generated research report or a client draft: every material claim is traced to its cited source, and each citation is reported as verified, broken, title-mismatched, or unverifiable. Gaps and confidence are stated, not smoothed over.

2. Vendor-Claim Verification — for organisations

The same check, scoped to one vendor's claims pack: the vendor's claims are checked against published evidence, claim by claim, using the same retracing method. This service does not certify a vendor — it reports what the evidence does and does not support.

We do not offer general strategy, implementation, training, or advisory retainers. Desk research only.

How it works

  1. You send the material and the question. One report, one draft, or one vendor's claims pack, with the question it is supposed to answer.
  2. We agree the pass criteria in writing, before any work. What counts as a cited, checkable claim, the scope and format, and what counts as a fail are written down and signed — they are not agreed by talking about them.
  3. We check it, then a human reviews it. The method is: question framing, evidence acquisition, claim-level traceability, adversarial review, human decision gates, and monitoring and correction. No report leaves without human review.
  4. You confirm acceptance or failure in writing within 5 working days of delivery. If it fails a criterion, one revision round is included.

What you get

  • A report or audit with every material claim sourced and cited.
  • A sourced-claim rate, gaps, and confidence stated plainly.
  • An Audit Certificate as page 1 of the delivered report.
  • A clear statement of what the evidence does NOT show, where that applies.
